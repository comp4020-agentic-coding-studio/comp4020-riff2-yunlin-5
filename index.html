A nonprofit, since 1980

The people behind TeX, LaTeX, and TeX Live

The TeX Users Group (TUG) is a member-funded nonprofit that keeps a decades-old typesetting system usable, current, and free. If you have ever opened a paper, thesis, or book with crisp mathematics and even, hand-set looking type, there is a good chance TeX did the setting, and TUG keeps the lights on for the people who maintain it.

What TUG actually does

Three things, mostly. Membership dues, not investors, pay for all of them.

Coordinates TeX Live

The distribution most people install to get a working TeX system on Linux, Windows, or macOS, maintained jointly with user groups worldwide and released once a year.

Publishes TUGboat

A journal of tutorials, technical articles, and community news that has run since the group was founded. Anyone can propose an article.

Runs the annual conference

Usually held alongside a sister user group, and open to everyone from a first LaTeX document to font engineering.

CTAN, the archive underneath all of it

The Comprehensive TeX Archive Network is the primary repository for TeX-related software on the internet, with many thousands of packages. Nearly every TeX installation pulls from it, and TUG helps keep it running.

TUG is a not-for-profit organisation by, for, and of its members

If you use any TeX-related program, consider joining TUG or one of the other user groups around the world. Memberships and donations are tax-deductible in the US, and every membership is for the calendar year no matter when you join.
